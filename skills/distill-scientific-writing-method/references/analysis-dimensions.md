# Nine analysis dimensions

## 1. Reader model

Infer what the author assumes the reader knows and accepts responsibility to explain. Distinguish disciplinary knowledge, specialized necessary concepts, manuscript-specific concepts, and implementation details.

## 2. Naming and vocabulary

Compare established terminology, direct physical descriptions, abbreviations, sample codes, variables, and author-created labels. Ask whether each term reduces reader burden or only author bookkeeping.

## 3. Information selection

Distinguish central findings, supporting evidence, secondary observations, method requirements, implementation details, intermediate outputs, artefacts, validation, and interesting but nonessential facts. Look for evidence of foregrounding, compression, relocation, and omission without treating absence alone as deliberate choice.

## 4. Detail and information budget

Compare available information with discussed information. Examine exact values, trends, ranges, representative points, extremes, definitions, caveats, method density, figure discussion density, and Supplementary Information routing. Infer how much evidence accompanies one claim and where discussion stops.

## 5. Defensive-writing policy

Analyse claim strength, hedging, limitations, alternative explanations, uncertainty, validation, caveats, and reviewer-facing defence. Examine words such as `shows`, `demonstrates`, `suggests`, `indicates`, `may`, `appears`, and `approximately` in context rather than counting them. Infer minimum sufficient defence and its stopping point.

## 6. Narrative architecture

Trace sequencing at paper, section, and paragraph levels. Record when the question, contribution, variables, design, structures, figures, mechanisms, implications, transitions, and recalls appear. Test whether observation normally precedes explanation and when essential theory legitimately precedes observation.

## 7. Sentence and paragraph functions

Analyse rhetorical jobs rather than superficial sentence length. Track orientation, claim placement, observation, quantification, interpretation, qualification, transition, topic sentences, and decomposition of complex reasoning. Extract functional patterns without copying wording.

## 8. Figure-led storytelling

Determine whether figures act as evidence repositories, narrative waypoints, explanatory devices, or combinations. Analyse order, panel density, messages per figure, later recall, caption/text division, omitted diagnostics, and table organization.

## 9. Anti-patterns

Retain only evidence-backed practices the author criticizes or recurrently avoids. Test possible anti-patterns such as unexplained labels, author-centered shorthand, numerical inventories, artefacts foregrounded as findings, implementation variables given scientific weight, repeated explanation, over-qualification, and showing everything known rather than everything needed.

## Governing questions

Repeatedly ask:

1. Why does this sentence exist?
2. Why is this information introduced here?
3. Why is this detail quantified but another is not?
4. Why is this concept named?
5. Why does this figure appear here?
6. Why does the discussion stop here?
7. What else could have been included?
8. What does the reader need now?

The goal is editorial judgement, not a list of prose statistics.
